Is the CRA obsolete already? Provocative, but we all see the trend...

Interesting paper by Víctor Mayoral-Vilches:
"𝐂𝐞𝐫𝐭𝐢𝐟𝐲𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐆𝐡𝐨𝐬𝐭𝐬: 𝐇𝐨𝐰 𝐂𝐲𝐛𝐞𝐫𝐬𝐞𝐜𝐮𝐫𝐢𝐭𝐲 𝐀𝐈 𝐀𝐠𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐬 𝐁𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐤 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐄𝐔 𝐂𝐲𝐛𝐞𝐫 𝐑𝐞𝐬𝐢𝐥𝐢𝐞𝐧𝐜𝐞 𝐀𝐜𝐭"
👉 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.07109 (arXiv: 2607.07109)

CRA is not bad: It doesn't demand zero vulnerabilities, it demands that manufacturers run a process (assess risk, handle flaws, ship updates).
The problem is that it's based on *some* premises that AI agents have (already) broken.

The key insight is about bending vs breaking:
* 𝐁𝐞𝐧𝐝𝐬: AI finds more vulnerabilities, faster. That's a volume problem. The CRA can absorb it. We can prioritise defensibly
* 𝐁𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐤𝐬: AI collapses the tempo and economics of the entire vulnerability lifecycle. The disclosure-to-weaponisation window has fallen from 771 days (2018) to effectively zero (2025).
When exploitation coincides with disclosure, a point-in-time certification becomes meaningless.

The paper's timeline is worth noting:
* the CRA's assumptions were frozen in September 2022.
* ChatGPT shipped ten weeks later.
* By the time the CRA fully applies in December 2027, AI agents had already exploited real one-day CVEs (2024), rediscovered latent flaws on demand (2024), and compromised CRA-scope consumer robots (2026).

As the paper puts it: the CRA "𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐜𝐡𝐞𝐬 𝐟𝐮𝐥𝐥 𝐟𝐨𝐫𝐜𝐞 𝐢𝐧 𝐃𝐞𝐜𝐞𝐦𝐛𝐞𝐫 𝟐𝟎𝟐𝟕 𝐜𝐞𝐫𝐭𝐢𝐟𝐲𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐝𝐮𝐜𝐭𝐬 𝐚𝐠𝐚𝐢𝐧𝐬𝐭 𝐚 𝐰𝐨𝐫𝐥𝐝 𝐭𝐡𝐚𝐭 𝐡𝐚𝐬 𝐚𝐥𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐝𝐲 𝐥𝐞𝐟𝐭 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐛𝐮𝐢𝐥𝐝𝐢𝐧𝐠".

It's a thought-provoking view, yet well argued IMO, how AI changes cybersecurity for long-lived embedded systems.
Worth a read.
